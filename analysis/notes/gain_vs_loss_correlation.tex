\noindent
This note defines the redundancy $\omega = t\,\rho_e$ (test fraction times the loss correlation
of two split predictors) and its study-only estimate $\widehat\omega^{\mathrm{study}}_k$, and
derives the reference curve $G^{\text{test}}_K \approx K/(1+(K-1)\,\omega)$ that relates it to the
variance-equivalent test sample gain. It replaces the former redundancy score
$\widehat C^{\mathrm{study}}_{g,k}\,\widehat\rho^{\mathrm{study}}_{e,k}\,\bar m^{\mathrm{study}}_k$,
and explains why an absolute early-stopping threshold can be placed on the redundancy but not on
the former score. Notation follows the
paper; results taken from it are restated so that this note reads on its own, with a pointer
to where they appear (section numbers of the current draft, appendix titles in full).

\section{Setting and definitions}

A study set of $N$ observations is split $K$ times by Monte-Carlo cross-validation (MCCV):
each split $a$ draws a test set $\mathcal{D}^{\text{te}}_a$ of size $n_{\text{te}}$ uniformly
without replacement, independently across splits, and trains the predictor
$g_a = F_\lambda(\mathcal{D}^{\text{tr}}_a,\xi_a)$ on the remaining
$n_{\text{tr}} = N - n_{\text{te}}$ observations. The test fraction is
\begin{equation}
  t = \frac{n_{\text{te}}}{N}.
\end{equation}
For an observation $i$ held out by split $a$, let
\begin{equation}
  d_{a,i} = \ell\bigl(g_a(x_i), y_i\bigr) - \mathcal{R}^*(g_a)
\end{equation}
be its per-sample loss centred at the risk of the predictor that did not see it, so that
$\mathbb{E}[d_{a,i} \mid g_a] = 0$, and let $\sigma^2_e = \mathbb{E}[d_{a,i}^2]$ be the
per-sample loss variance.

The benchmark-adjusted fold error of split $a$ is
$\delta_a = R_{\mathcal{D}^{\text{te}}_a}(g_a) - \widehat{R}^{\star}(g_a)$, and the $K$-split
estimation error is $\Delta_K = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{a=1}^K \delta_a$. When the evaluation noise
of the benchmark estimate $\widehat{R}^{\star}$ is negligible,
\begin{equation}
  \delta_a = \frac{1}{n_{\text{te}}} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}^{\text{te}}_a} d_{a,i}.
  \label{eq:delta-average}
\end{equation}
This is the evaluation-noise approximation of the paper (Section~3, paragraph ``Evaluation-noise
approximation''; Appendix~B, ``Measuring and approximating the evaluation noise''). With
$\sigma^{2,\mathrm{te}} = \operatorname{Var}(\delta_a)$ and
$\tau^{\mathrm{te}} = \operatorname{Cov}(\delta_a,\delta_b)$ for $a \neq b$, the exchangeability
of the splits gives
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Var}(\Delta_K) = \frac{1}{K}\,\sigma^{2,\mathrm{te}} + \frac{K-1}{K}\,\tau^{\mathrm{te}}.
  \label{eq:var-delta-K}
\end{equation}
(paper, Section~3, variance of $\Delta_K$; Appendix~C, ``Benchmark-adjusted fold errors'').

\section{Step 1: the gain depends on the fold-error correlation only}

The variance-equivalent test sample gain $G^{\text{test}}_K = N^{\mathrm{equiv}}_K/n_{\text{te}}$
is the factor by which a single hold-out test set must be enlarged to match the variance of
$\Delta_K$. When the single-split variance scales as $1/m$ with the test size $m$, it equals
the variance ratio
\begin{equation}
  G^{\text{test}}_K \approx \frac{\operatorname{Var}(\delta_a)}{\operatorname{Var}(\Delta_K)}
  = \frac{K}{1 + (K-1)\,\rho_\delta},
  \qquad
  \rho_\delta = \frac{\tau^{\mathrm{te}}}{\sigma^{2,\mathrm{te}}}
              = \operatorname{Corr}(\delta_a, \delta_b), \; a \neq b,
  \label{eq:gain-rho-delta}
\end{equation}
where the second equality follows from~\eqref{eq:var-delta-K}. This is the intraclass-correlation
form of the paper (Appendix~D.1, ``Benchmark-aware estimation of sample gain''); the gain itself
is defined in Section~3 (Definitions ``Variance-Equivalent Test Size'' and ``Test Sample Gain''). Up to the $1/m$ scaling, this
step is exact: everything the gain depends on is in the fold-error correlation $\rho_\delta$.

\section{Step 2: decomposing the fold-error covariance}

\paragraph{Single split.}
Given $g_a$, the held-out observations are i.i.d.\ and the $d_{a,i}$ are centred, so the cross
terms within a split vanish and
\begin{equation}
  \sigma^{2,\mathrm{te}} = \frac{\sigma^2_e}{n_{\text{te}}}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Two splits.}
For $a \neq b$, let $I_{ab} = \mathcal{D}^{\text{te}}_a \cap \mathcal{D}^{\text{te}}_b$ be the
observations held out by both. Expanding the product of the two averages
in~\eqref{eq:delta-average} and separating the shared observations from the pairs of distinct
observations,
\begin{equation}
  \tau^{\mathrm{te}}
  = \frac{1}{n_{\text{te}}^2}
    \Bigl(
      \underbrace{\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sum_{i \in I_{ab}} d_{a,i}\, d_{b,i}\Bigr]}_{\text{shared observations}}
      +
      \underbrace{\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}^{\text{te}}_a}\;
                  \sum_{j \in \mathcal{D}^{\text{te}}_b,\, j \neq i} d_{a,i}\, d_{b,j}\Bigr]}_{\text{train/test coupling}}
    \Bigr).
  \label{eq:tau-te-split}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Shared observations.}
Two independent uniform test sets share on average
$\mathbb{E}|I_{ab}| = n_{\text{te}}^2 / N = t\, n_{\text{te}}$ observations. On each of them
the losses of the two split predictors covary by $\rho_e\,\sigma^2_e$, where
\begin{equation}
  \rho_e = \frac{\mathbb{E}[d_{a,i}\, d_{b,i} \mid i \in I_{ab}]}{\sigma^2_e}
\end{equation}
is the correlation of the per-sample losses of two split predictors on an observation that
neither was trained on. The first term of~\eqref{eq:tau-te-split} is therefore
$t\, n_{\text{te}}\, \rho_e\, \sigma^2_e / n_{\text{te}}^2$.

\paragraph{Train/test coupling.}
For $i \neq j$, $d_{a,i}$ and $d_{b,j}$ are not independent: $i$ may belong to the training set
of $g_b$, $j$ to that of $g_a$, and $g_a$ and $g_b$ share most of their training data. This
dependence is intrinsic to cross-validation and is part of $\tau^{\mathrm{te}}$, as the paper
states when it introduces $\tau^{\mathrm{te}}$ (Section~3; Appendix~C, law of total covariance). Writing
$\gamma = \mathbb{E}[d_{a,i}\, d_{b,j}]$ for the average covariance of such a pair, the second
term is approximately $\gamma$, since there are about $n_{\text{te}}^2$ such pairs.

\paragraph{Result.}
Dividing by $\sigma^{2,\mathrm{te}} = \sigma^2_e / n_{\text{te}}$,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \rho_\delta = t\,\rho_e + \kappa,
  \qquad
  \kappa = \frac{n_{\text{te}}\,\gamma}{\sigma^2_e}.
  \;}
  \label{eq:rho-delta-decomp}
\end{equation}
The loss correlation $\rho_e$ measures how similarly two split predictors err on the same
observation. The factor $t$ is the share of a test set made of observations that another split
also tests. The coupling contribution $\kappa$ can have either sign.

\section{Step 3: the redundancy and the reference curve}

Substituting~\eqref{eq:rho-delta-decomp} into~\eqref{eq:gain-rho-delta},
\begin{equation}
  G^{\text{test}}_K \approx \frac{K}{1 + (K-1)\,(t\,\rho_e + \kappa)}.
  \label{eq:gain-full}
\end{equation}
We call
\begin{equation}
  \omega = t\,\rho_e
  \label{eq:redundancy}
\end{equation}
the \emph{redundancy}: the part of the fold-error correlation carried by observations that two
splits both test. With $\kappa = 0$, \eqref{eq:gain-full} is the reference curve
$K/(1+(K-1)\,\omega)$. Two limits
can be read directly. For $\rho_e \to 0$, split predictors err independently and
$G^{\text{test}}_K \to K$. For $\rho_e \to 1$, all split predictors err alike and
$G^{\text{test}}_K \to K/(1+(K-1)t)$, whose limit as $K\to\infty$ is
$1/t = N/n_{\text{te}}$: cross-validation then averages over the whole study set and no more.

\paragraph{Study-only estimate of the redundancy.}
After the first $k$ splits of a single run, let $I_{ab}$ be the observations held out by both
splits $a<b$ and $e_{a,i} = \ell(g_a(x_i), y_i)$ the per-sample loss, computed with the loss of
the metric whose gain is targeted. The pair-averaged loss covariance, loss variance and their
ratio are
\begin{align}
  \widehat C^{\mathrm{study}}_{e,k}
  &= \binom{k}{2}^{-1} \sum_{a<b} \widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}_{i\in I_{ab}}(e_{a,i},e_{b,i}), \\
  \widehat V^{\mathrm{study}}_{e,k}
  &= \binom{k}{2}^{-1} \sum_{a<b} \tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl(
      \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}_{i\in I_{ab}}(e_{a,i}) + \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}_{i\in I_{ab}}(e_{b,i})\Bigr), \\
  \widehat\rho^{\mathrm{study}}_{e,k} &= \widehat C^{\mathrm{study}}_{e,k} / \widehat V^{\mathrm{study}}_{e,k},
\end{align}
(these are the loss covariance, variance and correlation of the paper, Appendix~D.3,
``Study-only redundancy for early-stopping cross-validation''), and the redundancy after $k$ splits is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat\omega^{\mathrm{study}}_{k} = t\, \widehat\rho^{\mathrm{study}}_{e,k}.
  \label{eq:omega-hat}
\end{equation}
It is dimensionless and lies between $-t$ and $t$. It can be read after $k = 3$ or $k = 5$ splits:
configuration-level relations are the same, while the spread of a single run's reading roughly
halves from $k = 3$ to $k = 5$.
The empirical overlap $\bar m^{\mathrm{study}}_k / n_{\text{te}}$, with
$\bar m^{\mathrm{study}}_k$ the mean of $|I_{ab}|$ over pairs, may replace $t$: it has the same
expectation.

\section{Relation to the former redundancy score}

The former redundancy score of the paper (Appendix~D.3, boxed equation; Section~5.1, paragraph
``Measuring how sample gains settle in'') is
$\widehat s_k = \widehat C^{\mathrm{study}}_{g,k}\,
\widehat\rho^{\mathrm{study}}_{e,k}\, \bar m^{\mathrm{study}}_k$, where
$\widehat C^{\mathrm{study}}_{g,k}$ is the pair-averaged covariance of the two split
predictions on $I_{ab}$. Since $\mathbb{E}\,\bar m^{\mathrm{study}}_k = t\, n_{\text{te}}$,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat s_k \approx \widehat C^{\mathrm{study}}_{g,k}\; n_{\text{te}}\; \widehat\omega^{\mathrm{study}}_k .
\end{equation}
The former score thus contains the redundancy, multiplied by the prediction covariance, which
carries the squared units of the target and the strength of the signal, and by the test-set
size, which grows with the study. Both factors vary across settings independently of the gain.
The redundancy is dimensionless and bounded by $t$, which is why an absolute early-stopping
threshold can be placed on it. On the prospective holdout of the paper (216 configurations,
50 seeds) with gains on the mean squared error, the former score is not informative (correlation
with $\log G^{\text{test}}_{20}$ of $+0.11$ after 3 splits), while the redundancy is
($-0.60$ after 3 splits, $-0.67$ after 5).

\section{Assumptions and scope}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The evaluation noise of the benchmark estimate is negligible and the single-split
        variance scales as $1/m$ with the test size.
  \item $\widehat\rho^{\mathrm{study}}_{e,k}$, computed on $I_{ab}$ with sample centring,
        estimates $\rho_e$ with a bias of order $1/|I_{ab}|$; the held-out folds must therefore
        contain a few observations.
  \item The overlap $\mathbb{E}|I_{ab}| = t\,n_{\text{te}}$ holds for MCCV. For repeated
        $K$-fold, splits of the same repeat have disjoint test sets and the factor changes
        accordingly.
  \item The reference curve omits the coupling term $\kappa$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Empirical checks on simulated data}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The study-only $\widehat\rho^{\mathrm{study}}_{e,k}$ matches, within $1\%$ across all
        simulated designs, the population $\rho_e$ of the same split predictors measured on
        the benchmarking set.
  \item Varying the test fraction with everything else fixed, the fitted factor
        $\rho_\delta/\rho_e$ follows $t$ (Table~\ref{tab:factor}); fits with an intercept give
        intercepts within $\pm 0.01$.
  \item Regression configurations and the \texttt{PCam} networks lie on the reference curve
        (\texttt{PCam}: median $\log(G^{\text{test}}_{20}/\text{curve})$ between $-0.02$ and
        $+0.04$). Simulated classifiers fall $10$--$50\%$ below it at training sizes
        $100$--$300$, that is $\kappa > 0$ there, and the gap closes by
        $n_{\text{tr}} \approx 800$.
\end{enumerate}
